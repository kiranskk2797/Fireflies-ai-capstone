# Reference-image analysis

**Source reviewed:** all 12 supplied screenshots, the Step 3 solution-prioritization report, and the Step 4 assignment brief.

## Existing interface patterns to preserve

- **Fireflies shell:** dark charcoal surfaces, compact global search/header, narrow left navigation, restrained purple active navigation and primary-action accents, thin borders, and compact rounded task containers.
- **Tasks placement:** the relevant screenshots show Tasks as an existing left-navigation destination, with My Tasks / All Tasks switching, meeting-grouped task cards, completion controls, task wording, owner chips/avatars, and a “New Task” entry point. The proposed workflow belongs here rather than in a new project-management dashboard.
- **Information hierarchy:** meeting title and date identify each task group; task wording and completion state are the primary row information; owner identity is visible but secondary.
- **Reference uncertainty:** screenshots are compressed captures of specific screens and do not establish exact dimensions, responsive breakpoints, typography tokens, or behavior for candidate confirmation, deliberate drop, blockers, and accepted reassignment. The prototype follows visible patterns but does not claim pixel-perfect reproduction.

## New workflow elements

- A candidate/awaiting-confirmation label distinguishes extracted wording from a genuine commitment.
- Meeting source and a short context excerpt support owner review.
- Confirm, edit, decline, and suggest-another-owner actions preserve owner agency.
- A replacement owner has a separate acceptance step; suggesting a person does not assign work.
- Open, blocked, pending reassignment, and resolved outcomes are distinct. Blocked and pending are not resolved.
- Done, deliberate drop, and accepted reassignment show explicit outcomes. Simulated follow-up stops or changes with state.

## Screenshot-use boundary

The Tasks views guide the shell and task treatment. Screenshots of Home, Daily Brief, Analytics, Voice Agents, AI Skills, Meetings, and meeting details inform the surrounding product context only; the prototype does not reproduce those unrelated pages or imply their features are connected.
